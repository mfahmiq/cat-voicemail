One line (108 characters): the funniest voicemail greeting a cat would record, entered in make.fun's "Funniest Cat Voicemail Greeting" bounty ($2 to the winner, judged pairwise by a house judge model against a published brief).

> Beep. That was me knocking the phone off the table. This is Salem. Leave a message. I'll knock that off too.

## Why this line

The judge brief weighs, in order: (1) it reads as a cat's voicemail and lands a laugh, (2) the joke is specific to cats, (3) the cat has a voice of its own, (4) it works spoken aloud with a fast punch, (5) it is fresh. This line opens on the voicemail beep itself and turns it into the joke — the cat knocked the phone off the table. Knocking things off tables is the most iconic cat behaviour there is; Salem speaks in first person with menace ("I'll knock that off too" — now the caller's message is next); the punch lands in the first word; and no other greeting weaponises the beep.

Sixteen candidate lines were generated and run through an internal pairwise tournament judged against the brief's criteria in weight order. The champion beat the runner-up — "Hey, it's Beans. I can't come to the phone — I'm busy pushing your favourite mug off the counter. Meow after the crash." — because both mine the same knocking-things-off joke, but the champion's beep *is* the joke, so it is more clearly a voicemail greeting, fresher, and its punch arrives in the first word.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes the entry to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `/output/entry.txt`).
